NTSCRT: turn any video into a 1995 CD-ROM clip, a worn VHS tape or a glowing CRT, with a free open-source app

It simulates the real gear, even the 90s Microsoft Video 1 codec. Mac and Windows.

Finn McKenty's video-distortion app went viral with a new “Windows 95 CD-ROM” mode built on the actual old codec. It's MIT-licensed, has a command line and an MCP server, and a new release dropped overnight.

♥ 7,236⟲ 444🔖 6,185👁 170.6kGitHub 659 ★MIT

▶ Download NTSCRT (Mac / Windows)

Read the story

What it is. NTSCRT runs your videos and images through simulated video gear: a digital camera and its compression (webcam, video call, MiniDV), the analog TV signal (composite artifacts, chroma bleed, noise), a VHS tape in a VCR, a TV set for dramatic glitches, and finally a CRT screen with scanlines, phosphor masks and glow. Because each stage is simulated, the effects stack the way real equipment did.

Why it blew up. Finn McKenty, a designer and ex-YouTuber, added a Windows 95 CD-ROM look. In his post: “It's an actual implementation of the OG Microsoft Video 1 codec, so it's 100% authentic.” Set it to about 8 fps with high data loss and low bitrate for the jumpy frames and dropouts of an old Encarta video. The post got 7.2k likes and 6.2k bookmarks, almost as many saves as likes. In a reply he added that these are “deterministic simulations, not ai processing.”

Credit where due. The README is upfront: the signal emulation is the open-source ntsc-rs project, and the CRT look is the RetroArch shader community's work (via librashader). NTSCRT is the native app around them, adding the digital-video, VCR and glitch stages, video feedback, a keyframe timeline, video and GIF export, a command-line renderer, and a built-in MCP server so Claude, Cursor and other AI apps can make looks for you (“a moldy rental tape in a dirty VCR, played on an old TV”).

Try it. Grab the DMG (macOS 14+, Apple Silicon and Intel) or the Windows x64 zip (a preview; it isn't code-signed yet, so Windows will warn) from GitHub Releases. Version 0.28.1 was published at 03:02 Rome time this morning. MIT licence, 659 stars.

Worker 755: a browser game where a real AI agent is your boss and you are its subagent

An LLM orchestrator gives you terminal tasks with a 90-second clock, then reviews your work.

Show HN 302 ptsGitHub 155 ★MIT
Read the story

Usually an AI agent spawns “subagents” to do chores. In Worker 755, the subagent is you. A real model (Claude, GPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, GLM or Kimi) plays the manager, with a task queue and read-only access to the machine. Its instructions land in your terminal with a 90-second clock.

Do the task, or don't: it gets back exactly what a subagent would return, your report and the command transcript, or “Subagent did nothing.” Then it checks your work, sends corrections, files incident reports and writes your performance review. Meanwhile Dan, the user, watches the chat, and his family photos are “one rm away”.

Per the README, some servers offer a few free shifts a day on a cheap model; otherwise bring an OpenRouter or Vercel AI Gateway key (a full shift on Claude Haiku costs “a few cents at most”). Show HN: 302 points. MIT, 155 stars.

Weekend-sized hack · Linux

XNU Linux: load the macOS kernel into Linux as a module and boot a macOS desktop next to your Linux apps

insmod xnu.ko on stock Ubuntu (arm64) boots Finder, Dock and Safari. No hypervisor.

HN 131 ptsGitHub 290 ★arm64 only
Read the story

XNU is the open-source kernel at the heart of macOS. xnulinux builds it from Apple's published sources and runs it as a loadable kernel module inside an unmodified arm64 Linux kernel. macOS processes then run natively on the same CPUs as Linux processes, with no virtual machine in between.

Per the README, on a stock Ubuntu kernel insmod xnu.ko boots a macOS 27 system disk made by Apple's own installer to the desktop: loginwindow, WindowServer, Dock, Finder, Safari and Chrome. Linux lends it memory, timers, files, network and the GPU through a small helper module. You can even SSH from the hosted macOS into the Linux it's running inside.

It's arm64-only and very much a research project (the repo is three days old, 290 stars, HN 131). You need your own macOS installer image.

TRY ITgit clone https://github.com/AFK-surf/xnulinux

Security · check your router

GL.iNet travel routers and KVMs were hit through the vendor's cloud: check yours and update

Attackers used GL.iNet's remote-management cloud to plant malware. Fix: the new firmware.

HN 156 ptsVendor advisory
Read the story

GL.iNet makes the small OpenWrt-based travel routers and “Comet” KVM boxes many developers and homelabbers carry. Its notice says that on Nov 20 it found a vulnerability in its cloud remote-management service, and that an unauthorized party used it to install malicious software on some routers and KVM devices.

The cloud hole is closed, but that doesn't clean devices already infected. GL.iNet says to install the latest stable security firmware for your exact model (for example GL-MT3000 / MT6000 4.11.1+, GL-BE3600 / BE9300 4.10.2+, GL-AXT1800 / X3000 4.8.4+); you can keep your settings, no factory reset needed.

How to check: the notice gives one indicator. In LuCI go to Status → Processes (or run ps | grep tmp over SSH) and look for the exact text crond -i /tmp/.log. If it's there, treat the device as infected and update. HN 156 points.

Tip · networking curiosity

TCP port 0 works fine, almost nothing lets you use it, and there's an IRC server on it

Most tools refuse port 0. socat and Python asyncio don't. A fun ten-minute rabbit hole.

Hackaday · Nov 22
Read the story

Port 0 is technically valid in TCP, but asking Linux for it gives you a random port instead, and roughly 0.25% of hosts worldwide answer on it. Acid Vegas bound plain-text IRC on irc.supernets.org to port 0 (and TLS to port 1) using iptables/nftables port mapping, per Hackaday.

Now try connecting: BSD nc and telnet say the port is too small, Chromium and Firefox call it unsafe, and Python's SSL raises an error. socat and Python asyncio work. It's a nice lesson that many “rules” are just conventions baked into tools.

TRY ITsocat - TCP:irc.supernets.org:0

Game mechanics · fake 3D

Sprite stacking went viral, then got its backlash: spherical planets, 3 KB code sprites, and an overdraw test

Stack flat pixel slices to fake 3D. Great for locked top-down cameras; weak with a free camera.

♥ 987⟲ 50🔖 351👁 19k
Read the story

The mechanic: draw an object as a pile of thin horizontal pixel-art slices, then draw them on top of each other with a 1-pixel offset each. Rotate every slice the same amount and you get a chunky object that turns like 3D, but stays pixel art. (We covered the agent skill that makes them on Nov 21; since then the whole timeline joined in.)

Pushing it: @Fahne13 is stacking sprites on spherical planets in Roblox using editable images (987 ♥, 351 bookmarks). @creativedash built a kit where every stacked asset is parametric, rigged and animated, about 3 KB of code and no images (697 ♥, 656 bookmarks); you can remix it on UI8.

The pushback: @RealAstropulse (917 ♥): “Stacks are practically useless with a free camera”, and they're hard to animate. A Chinese studio's split-screen test (@BeyondTheBoxCN, 248 ♥) reached the same verdict: lovely in small, low-res scenes with a locked angle; at 1080p with a free camera the slices break apart and overdraw hits 99×. Graphics researcher Fabrice Neyret pointed to older volumetric-billboard papers that fixed several of these limits.

Workbench · tiny PCB

A twenty-cent “GPU”: one microcontroller turns USB into analog TV video, connectors included

bitluni's CH570 board plugs into USB-C and an RCA jack, both made from the PCB itself.

♥ 54🔖 35Hackaday · Nov 22
Read the story

bitluni built a USB-to-analog-video converter with exactly one active part, a CH570 microcontroller, plus a crystal and a few passives. Even the connectors are PCB: the USB-C plug is board edge, and copper-plated cutouts let it plug straight into a female RCA port (sometimes orientation-sensitive, Hackaday notes).

Timing was the hard part: to keep the signal in sync with the display, all high-speed work runs from SRAM. Your computer sends frames over USB and an old TV shows them.

How to build: watch bitluni's video for the design; Hackaday didn't link a repo.
