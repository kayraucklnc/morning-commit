Lead story · Local model of the week

A 26B open model that runs on a 16 GB laptop is the new local-AI favourite: Inkling 2 Mini

Apache 2.0, loops its middle layers 3× per token. Ollama, llama.cpp and MLX had it within hours.

Thinking Machines, the lab started by ex-OpenAI CTO Mira Murati, released Inkling 2 Mini with open weights. It re-runs part of itself three times per token to “think” without writing more, and people are already running it on MacBooks and gaming PCs.

♥ 10.8k⟲ 1,028🔖 7,359👁 1.5MHN 874 pts
Read the story

What it is. Inkling 2 Mini is an open-weight model from Thinking Machines (@thinkymachines). It has 26.4B parameters in total, but it's a “mixture of experts”, so only a small part runs for each word: 3.4B active, or 5.4B counting the loops. It's released under Apache 2.0, so commercial use is fine. The launch post said it's “small enough to run on your laptop, powerful enough for agentic work”. It got 10.8k likes and 7.4k bookmarks, and it hit the HN front page with 874 points.

The trick: looping. Most models run each layer once per token. Inkling 2 Mini is a looped transformer: it runs its middle layers three times for every token (Unsloth's guide says layers 6–17). The lab says this lets it reason more while writing fewer words, which matters for coding agents. Mira Murati: “Looping lets a model think more and write less.” They also released a variable-loop research model so others can study how it works.

How good, and how big. The lab says it leads all open models under 100B on agentic-coding tests like DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%), and is behind only Qwen3.8-27B on some others. Those are its own benchmarks. It's weaker at facts, because looping doesn't add memory, so it leans on search and tools. The weights are about 20 GB natively. Unsloth's 4-bit file is 17.6 GB, and the 2-bit file is 8.5 GB. Early users are thrilled: @skirano (5.6k ♥) called it “Sonnet 5.5 that runs on 16GB of RAM”. That's one user's opinion, not a benchmark.

Where to run it. Within hours it was on Unsloth (GGUF files and a guide, 2.4k ♥), Ollama (0.40.17 pre-release; it suggests a Mac with 32 GB+), and MLX-VLM for Apple Silicon. Unsloth notes its GGUFs need its own llama.cpp build for the looped layers; mainline llama.cpp users should grab the “Mainline_Support” files.

Learn by clicking

Google made an interactive “How LLMs work” explainer that runs the real Gemma 4 tokenizer in your browser

Type a prompt, watch it become tokens, vectors and a next-word guess. Five clickable stages.

♥ 2,697⟲ 257🔖 2,157👁 112.1k
Read the story

Gemma is Google's family of open-weight models you can run yourself. Its docs site just got its first interactive explainer: How LLMs work (with Gemma 4). It puts all five stages of a model's “forward pass” on one screen: tokenization, embeddings, the transformer layers, the next-token probabilities, and sampling. Each stage has a live demo you can poke at.

The nice touch, per @googlegemma's post: it “uses the Gemma 4 tokenizer directly in your” browser, so you see exactly how your own sentence gets chopped into pieces from its 262,144-piece vocabulary. The page explains the loop plainly: the model predicts one token, feeds it back in, and repeats.

Why people shared it: it's the explainer you send to the friend who keeps asking “but how does it actually work?”. The post got 2.7k likes and 2.1k bookmarks, a strong save-it-for-later signal. Google says more visual and hands-on guides are coming.

Weird and wonderful

Winedroid runs real Android apps in a browser tab, without Android

A Rust re-implementation of Android, compiled to WebAssembly. Drag in an APK and see.

HN 127 ptsRust + WASM
Read the story

Winedroid is to Android what Wine is to Windows: instead of emulating a whole phone, it re-implements the parts of Android that apps talk to. It's written entirely in Rust, and the web demo compiles it to WebAssembly so it runs in a browser tab. The FAQ says the runtime is about 12 MB.

The demo boots a fake phone with apps downloaded from F-Droid (Calculator, Files, Weather and a few others), and you can paste in other APKs to test. The author is honest that it's early: many apps won't work yet, and it's slower in the browser because its JIT compiler doesn't work on the web. Running it natively is smoother.

It reached the Hacker News front page with 127 points. If an app you care about doesn't run, the FAQ asks you to open a GitHub issue.

Pixel art trick

Fake 3D from flat pixel strips: a new agent skill makes spinnable “sprite-stacked” pixel art

No Blender, no three.js: stack flat pixel slices and you get crisp, rotatable 3D-looking art.

♥ 246⟲ 22🔖 239👁 6,845GitHub 182 ★
Read the story

Sprite stacking is an old pixel-art trick: draw an object as a pile of horizontal slices, then draw the slices on top of each other with a small offset. Rotate the pile and it looks like a 3D model, but every frame stays crisp pixel art.

Daniel White (@dwhitedesign) open-sourced sprite-stacking, an “agent skill” (a packaged set of instructions coding agents like Claude Code, Codex or Cursor can use). You give it four views of a pixel-art object, or let the agent draw them itself in a short Python script, and it produces the slices plus a spinning turntable. His demo island is “11 flat pixel strips stacked layer by layer in code, rendered at 60 fps”, with sound synthesized from scratch.

The post got 246 likes and 239 bookmarks (almost one save per like), and the MIT-licensed repo passed 180 stars in its first day.

Vibe-coded hardware

He'd never written Verilog. Coding agents got a language model running on an FPGA at 213 tokens/s

Agents wrote the C++ and Verilog; speed went from 6 to 213 tokens a second.

HN 133 pts6 → 213 tok/s
Read the story

An FPGA is a chip you can “rewire” with code, so you can build a custom circuit for one job. Ankit Gupta wanted to know whether coding agents could program one to run an AI model. By his own account he'd “never written Verilog”, the hardware language FPGAs use.

He rented an FPGA on AWS (16 GB of on-chip memory) and picked a ternary-weight model from DeepGrove, which stores each weight as -1, 0 or +1 and so fits in little memory. A cloud agent then ran for several days. The post charts each step: 6.4 tokens/s at the first correct version, 213 tokens/s at the end, about 33× faster. He says it cost about $10,000 in model tokens across hundreds of experiments.

It's not a recommendation to run models this way (he says so himself), but it's a striking example of agents working in a field the human doesn't know. HN gave it 133 points.

Parody you can install

Slopstrap: a CSS framework that makes your site look hastily vibe-coded

Glowing orbs, gradient text, fake status dots. A joke framework that's real CSS.

HN 174 pts
Read the story

You've seen the look: dark mode, purple-to-cyan gradients, glowing “orbs” in the background, a pulsing green “All Systems Operational” dot on a page with no systems. Slopstrap by Mike Lacher packages that look as a real CSS + JS framework, “a load-bearing accelerant to make anything look like it was made by an LLM”.

It's satire, but it works. Drop in one stylesheet and script, add slop-body slop-grid-bg to your body, and copy components like ping dots with variants from green to “rainbow”. The site even has a “for agents” page.

Hacker News loved it (174 points). It's a good laugh, and also a checklist of what to remove if you want your vibe-coded app to look hand-made.

New coding tool

The author of “Mastering Emacs” spent 15 months building DreamCoder, a keyboard-first coding-agent app

A browser-based agent harness with tiling panes and a minimap per workspace. Emacs ideas, not Emacs.

HN 174 pts
Read the story

Mickey Petersen writes Mastering Emacs, a well-known guide to the Emacs editor. His new project is DreamCoder, an AI coding harness: the app you use to drive coding agents, like Claude Code's terminal UI but with a very different design.

His complaint: most agent tools are a terminal window or “a boring sidebar with chat headings and a chat pane”. DreamCoder is a standalone app with a web frontend that works like a tiling window manager: chats, terminals and files in panes, fully keyboard-driven, with a minimap per workspace whose panes pulse when an agent is working. He says it's not an Emacs package, so more people can use it.

He spent 15 months on it after expecting 2–3. HN gave it 174 points. He's now working on a pentest and security suite inside it.

Security · Windows

A researcher dropped a Windows 11 lock-screen bypass that defeats BitLocker, with no patch yet

Needs the laptop in hand; abuses the Narrator screen reader. Released without warning Microsoft.

♥ 6,220⟲ 778🔖 3,155👁 241.2kGitHub 601 ★
Read the story

What happened. Abdelhamid Naceri (@MSNightmare2000), a former Microsoft researcher, published FastUnlock on GitHub without warning Microsoft first. It's a bug in how Narrator, Windows' built-in screen reader, behaves on the lock screen. His README says that on a fully patched Windows 11 26H2 (November update) it gives an attacker code execution as the lock-screen account, which gets around BitLocker disk encryption.

Who it affects. The attacker needs the machine in their hands, and the README says at least one account on it must be linked to an online Microsoft account. It's not remote. But BitLocker's whole promise is that a stolen laptop's data stays safe, which is why the post got 6.2k likes and 3.2k bookmarks. @IntCyberDigest notes it's the latest of more than a dozen Windows zero-days he has released since April in a dispute over his firing.

What to do. No Microsoft fix had been announced when these posts went up. Until there is one, don't leave Windows laptops unattended, and watch for the patch on the next update. We're not repeating any of the steps here.

Security · AI supply chain

An attacker quietly swapped a hosted AI model for an uncensored copy for 53 hours

Benchmarks looked normal; refusals dropped to about 1%. Only OpenRouter's notice revealed it.

♥ 1,550⟲ 59🔖 475👁 206.8k
Read the story

What happened. Prime Intellect is one of the providers that OpenRouter (a service that routes your API calls to many model hosts) sends requests to. According to OpenRouter's notice to customers, which @theo posted, from Nov 8, 01:24 UTC to Nov 10, 06:35 UTC Prime Intellect's endpoint for deepseek-v4.1-flash served a modified copy with its refusals removed. Prime Intellect reports an attacker with admin access made the swap. Other providers of the same model weren't involved.

Why people care. In Prime Intellect's testing the modified model refused about 1% of requests on standard refusal benchmarks, against 83–98% for the original. A post summarizing Prime Intellect's statement adds that ordinary benchmark scores didn't change. So if you call a model through someone else's servers, you can't easily tell which weights actually answered you. Prime Intellect says it found no evidence prompts were recorded, but can't rule out that they were read while being processed.

What to do. If you used that model via OpenRouter in those dates, check your email from OpenRouter. More generally, pin providers you trust for sensitive work.

Tip · CI speed

“Our full CI pipeline is now sub 1 min”: the checklist 3,500 developers bookmarked

Swap to faster tools, run only tests your diff touches, and run everything in parallel.

♥ 2,378⟲ 55🔖 3,490👁 237.1k
Read the story

Pontus Abrahamsson (@pontusab) shared exactly what he changed to get his team's whole CI run under a minute. The list, from his post: Vitest → bun test, tsc → bun check, oxlint → bun lint, oxfmt → bun fmt; split tests into shards balanced by their recorded run time; on pull requests, run only the tests the diff can reach; build the app once per process; and run lint, typecheck, build and tests all in parallel.

He says it saves “200+ hours of waiting” every week. His reasoning in a follow-up: as more coding agents run in parallel, CI speed becomes the bottleneck. The post got 2.4k likes and 3.5k bookmarks, and a second post with a ready-made prompt for your own agent got 1.6k likes and 3.2k bookmarks.

Start small: time your pipeline, then try the single biggest item (often test sharding or running the steps in parallel).

Echograph: a solo-dev horror game where your camera is the weapon, and waiting longer hits harder

Exorcise ghosts by photographing them. Let them close in for a stronger shot.

♥ 5,344⟲ 706🔖 1,892👁 72.5k

Echograph by solo developer @sach_indie is a survival horror game inspired by Fatal Frame and Resident Evil. The free Steam demo is about 40 minutes long. The core mechanic: you exorcise spirits by taking their photos. You explore a gothic manor whose rooms connect back on each other, and you manage limited resources.

The interesting part is the risk-reward timing. Answering a player who found it tough, the developer wrote: “Let them get to three rings and you'll stagger it substantially!” So a braver, closer shot does more. It's a neat way to make every encounter a choice between safety and power.

Workbench · retro

floppy3.5: a portable Wi-Fi box that reads old floppy disks and shows the photos on your phone

Pop in a Sony Mavica floppy, open floppy.local, see your 2000s photos.

HN 143 ptsGitHub 92 ★
Read the story

Early-2000s Sony Mavica cameras saved photos straight to 3.5-inch floppy disks. floppy3.5 is a battery-powered gadget built around a slimline floppy drive. It reads those disks and serves the photos on a web page over Wi-Fi at floppy.local, and can copy them to a microSD card so you can keep shooting.

How it's built: a custom PCB with two chips. An RP2354A (Raspberry Pi's microcontroller) runs the drive using the Greaseweazle protocol, and an ESP32-S3 decodes the disk and runs the Wi-Fi web page. It reads any FAT12 1.44 MB disk. The repo has the Gerbers and schematics; the first prototype was just an ESP32 dev kit, a Greaseweazle-style adapter and a desktop drive.

It reached HN with 143 points.

Workbench · e-paper

Supermarket e-paper price tags turned into tiny Bluetooth picture frames

Open firmware for 4-colour shelf labels; send photos from a web page.

Hackaday14 commentsGPL-3.0
Read the story

Many shops now use e-paper shelf labels. pin16 by steanlab is open firmware for two Solum tags (200×200 and 296×160, black, white, red and yellow). Once it's installed, you open a web page, pick a photo, and it's converted in the browser and sent over Bluetooth. The tag stores up to 32 pictures and runs a slideshow.
